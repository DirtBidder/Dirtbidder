B.5 Schedule, Weather and Delays

  • The Operator will start and finish within the timeline in the accepted bid, and will tell the Client promptly when something is going to push that timeline. B.6 Site Access and What the Client Must Disclose

B.7 Utility Locates and Permits

B.8 Unexpected Site Conditions

Rock, groundwater, buried debris or structures, unsuitable or contaminated soil, and undisclosed utilities are examples.

  • Neither user may require the other to absorb the cost of that condition. The Operator may submit a change request covering the added work, and the Client may approve it, decline it, or end the job under B.15. B.9 Material, Spoil and Haul-Off

B.14 Payment, Release and Lien Rights

  • The Client pays the bid price through the Site as Part A describes, and the Operator looks to the held funds for payment of the accepted bid and approved change requests.

  • Section 9 of Part A sets out what release means, and both users should understand it. Releasing a payment, whether the Client releases it or it releases automatically, discharges the Client’s payment obligation for that stage. It is not an inspection, not an acceptance of the quality of the work, and not a waiver of the Client’s rights under B.13 or of any other claim the Client has against the Operator.

  • Earthwork is an improvement to real property, so an Operator who is not paid may generally place a lien on the Client’s land. That is a real risk for a Client who has already paid, and the next three paragraphs are how this Part B handles it.

  • When a payment is released, the Operator waives, for the work and material that payment covers, every mechanic’s lien, bond claim, and similar claim it has or could have against the property, the Client, and the Client’s lender. The Operator will obtain the same waiver from every person it hires or buys material from for the job, and will give the Client copies on request. If someone in that position asserts a lien or claim against the property for work a released payment covered, the Operator will get it removed or bonded off at its own cost within fourteen (14) days of the Client asking, and will indemnify the Client for the cost of dealing with it, including reasonable attorneys’ fees.

  • Lien rights depend on notices, registrations, and filings that vary from state to state. Some of them must be given before the work starts or within days of starting, rather than when a payment is missed, and in some states a contractor that misses one loses its lien rights entirely, however much it is owed. Which ones apply can turn on who the Operator contracted with, whether the property is a home, and whether the Operator uses subcontractors or suppliers.

  • Those obligations belong to the Operator. The Operator is responsible for knowing which requirements apply where the site is, for meeting them, and for meeting them on time, and the Operator should get its own advice rather than relying on anything it reads here or on the Site. DirtBidder does not give any notice, make any filing or posting on any registry, track any deadline, or warn either user that one is approaching. An Operator that loses lien rights, and a Client that faces a claim because someone did or did not take one of these steps, has no claim against DirtBidder for it. Sections 22, 23 and 24 of Part A apply.
