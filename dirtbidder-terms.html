1. Who we are and what DirtBidder does

DirtBidder LLC ("DirtBidder," "we," "us") runs the website dirtbidder.com (the "Service"). The Service lets people who need earthwork done ("Clients") post jobs and lets equipment operators and contractors ("Operators") bid on them.

DirtBidder is a marketplace, not a contractor. We don't perform, supervise, direct or guarantee any work. We don't employ Operators, and Operators are not our agents. When a Client accepts a bid, the agreement for the work is between the Client and the Operator. Our role is to host the listing, carry the bids, hold payment in escrow through our payment processor, and decide disputes about escrowed funds as described below.

By creating an account or using the Service, you agree to these Terms and to our Privacy Policy. If you don't agree, don't use the Service.

3. Posting jobs

4. Bidding

  • Bids are sealed. Operators can't see each other's amounts.
  • A bid is an offer to do the described work for the stated price. When a Client accepts a bid and pays into escrow, the Client and the Operator have an agreement for that work at that price, on the terms in the job post and the bid.
  • Clients aren't required to accept any bid, including the lowest.
  • Changes to scope or price after hiring must be agreed by both sides. The escrowed amount covers only the accepted bid.

7. Fees

Posting a job and bidding are free. When a job is paid, DirtBidder charges a service fee to both the Client and the Operator. The fee is calculated in brackets, like income tax brackets, so larger jobs pay a lower overall rate:

8. Payment and escrow

  • Payments are processed by Stripe, Inc. By paying or receiving payouts you also agree to Stripe's terms. DirtBidder never sees or stores your full card or bank numbers.
  • When a Client hires an Operator, the Client pays the bid amount plus the Client fee. That money is held, and not paid to the Operator, until it's released as described in section 9 or decided as described in section 10.
  • Escrowed money earns no interest for either party.
  • Operators must set up a Stripe payout account to be paid. If an Operator hasn't finished setup when money is released, we hold their payout until they do.

9. Completion and release

  • When the work is done, the Operator marks the job complete.
  • The Client then has 72 hours to either release the payment or report a problem.
  • If the Client does neither within 72 hours, the payment is released to the Operator automatically.
  • Once payment is released, the Operator's payout (the bid amount minus the Operator fee) is sent to their bank through Stripe. A released payment can't be reversed through DirtBidder.

10. Disputes

  • A Client can report a problem before payment is released. The payment is then frozen.
  • Both sides can explain their position and submit photos. DirtBidder reviews what's submitted and decides how the escrowed money is handled. We can pay the Operator in full, refund the Client in full, or split the amount.
  • Our decision covers only the escrowed money. It isn't a legal ruling. Either party keeps any rights they have against the other under the law, such as small-claims court, for anything beyond it.
  • If the money is split, the Operator fee is recalculated on the amount the Operator actually receives.

11. Cancellations and refunds

  • A Client can close a job at no cost any time before hiring.
  • After payment, if both sides agree to cancel before work starts, contact us and we'll refund the Client, including the Client fee.
  • If work has started and the parties don't agree, it's handled as a dispute under section 10.
