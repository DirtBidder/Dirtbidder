Iowa Lien Deadlines

What operators need to know · October 24, 2026

If you do earthwork in Iowa and you are not paid, you can usually put a lien on the property. What catches people out is that you can lose that right before anything goes wrong, by missing a deadline that starts on your first day on site.

On DirtBidder, you are a general contractor

When a client hires you here, you contract directly with the property owner. Under Iowa law that makes you a general contractor, not a subcontractor, even on a small job and even if it is just you and your machine. The rules below are the ones that apply to general contractors.

Two things to do on any job at someone’s home

  1. Post a notice of commencement to the Mechanics’ Notice and Lien Registry within ten (10) days of starting work. Ten (10) days from the day you start, not from the day a payment goes wrong. (Iowa Code section 572.13A.)

  2. Give the owner the written notice set out in Iowa Code section 572.13. The statute prescribes the wording and requires a bold type of at least ten points.

Miss one and you have no lien at all

The statute says a general contractor who fails is “not entitled to a lien and remedy provided by this chapter.” Not a reduced lien. No lien, whatever you are owed. And a contractor who skips the notice and tries to lien anyway can face the owner's claim for damages and attorney fees on top.

Do both even if you work alone, and even if you are not sure the job counts

Two lines in the law are blurry, and both of them resolve the same way in practice.

The owner notice is written to apply to a general contractor who uses a subcontractor. The commencement notice reads as applying to every general contractor, whether or not you use one. If you work alone, you may be outside the first and inside the second.

“Residential” in Iowa means a single-family or two-family dwelling or a condominium. Whether that covers a pond, a driveway or a building pad on acreage is not obvious.

You do not need to work out which side of either line your job falls on. Do both, every time, on any job at a place somebody lives. Posting costs a small fee and ten minutes. Getting it wrong costs the entire lien.

Filing the lien itself

A lien is perfected by posting a verified statement of account to the same registry. The outside deadline is two (2) years and ninety (90) days after your last labor or material on the job. The ninety-day (90-day) mark still matters: a lien posted after it only reaches what the owner still owes, so a late lien against a fully paid owner can be worth nothing.

Waivers

Part B of the Terms requires you to waive your lien rights for the work each released payment covers, and to get the same waiver from anyone you hire or buy material from. If one of your suppliers liens your client's property for a job you were already paid for, clearing it is your cost.

This is yours, not DirtBidder’s

DirtBidder does not give these notices, does not post anything to the registry, does not track your deadlines, and will not warn you that one is coming up. The Terms say so. If you are unsure whether a requirement applies to your job, ask your own attorney, not us.
